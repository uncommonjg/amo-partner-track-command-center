Pitch Asset — PA Cold Email (Net New)

PA Program — Net New Cold Intro

PA · Net New
Subject Line Options
A Clinical placement infrastructure in [State] — built, proven, available
B Rotation coverage for [Program Name] — worth 20 minutes
C (Probation) Accreditation-ready rotation blocks in [State] — here when you need them
Hi [First Name], I'm reaching out because [Program] is in a market where AMO has built something that might be worth your time. We manage a clinical hub in the [Southeast / Southwest]: a curated, credentialed preceptor network across the core specialties PA programs need most. Schools use it to lock in rotation blocks for the full academic year rather than sourcing individual placements semester by semester. The short version: your students could rotate in this market without relocating. The network is already built. We're not building it for you specifically, but it fits, and we have open capacity. For programs navigating ARC-PA requirements, the value gets more specific: documented, guaranteed placement with a 45-day preceptor replacement SLA in writing. That's the kind of infrastructure that shows up in accreditation reviews. Would it be worth a 20-minute call to see what's available in [State / Metro]? [Name] AMO | Clinical Core [Phone]
Personalization Notes
  • Probation programs: emphasize the SLA and accreditation documentation language. That's their specific pain.
  • New/provisional programs: emphasize "the network is already built — you don't have to build it." They're starting from zero.
  • HBCU programs (Morehouse, NC A&T): mission alignment: underserved communities, diverse preceptor network angle.
  • UT Rio Grande Valley: dean-level pitch: enrollment reduction risk is a tuition revenue story. Use the enrollment/growth angle.
  • Do NOT mention specific specialties in the first email. Get the meeting.
Pitch Asset — DO Cold Email (Net New)

DO Program — Net New Cold Intro

DO · Net New
Subject Line Options
A DO rotation capacity in [State] — packaged and available
B Clinical year infrastructure for [Program] — let's talk
C (New programs) Preceptor network for [Program] — already built in your market
Hi [First Name], AMO manages clinical rotation networks in markets where DO programs often struggle to source specialty-matched preceptors. [State] is one of them. I'm reaching out specifically because we have preceptor inventory in your region that's structured for DO students: no student relocation required, specialty coverage that aligns with COCA requirements, and preceptors who've worked with DO programs before. [For new programs:] For programs that are still building clinical partnerships, AMO functions as the infrastructure layer behind your clinical year. You lock in an academic-year commitment; we guarantee coverage and manage all preceptor documentation and credentialing. [For established programs:] For programs looking to add reliability to an existing network, especially in specialties that are harder to source regionally, AMO can fill the gaps without adding internal coordinator workload. Would it make sense to walk through what's available in [State / Region] for AY 2027–28? [Name] AMO | Clinical Core [Phone]
Personalization Notes
  • DO programs are more conservative. Tone is warmer and more institutional, not sales-y. Show you know the market.
  • New campuses (Burrell FL, VCOM-Louisiana): use the "infrastructure layer" framing. They're building from scratch.
  • Large programs (LMU-DCOM, TCOM): lead with volume and coordination complexity. "At your scale, placement logistics don't have to be internal."
  • VCOM campuses: approach at the system level, not campus level. Reference the distributed rotation model explicitly.
  • Do NOT use "staffing service" language. Do NOT address PAs or DOs as "Dr." unless they have a doctorate. Use "Director" or their specific title.
  • Do NOT lead with OMM. AMO has minimal OMM inventory; if they ask, that's an NDS/build conversation.
